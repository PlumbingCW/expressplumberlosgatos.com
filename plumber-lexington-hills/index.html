What to check when the water pressure drops

.

On a well or spring system, these steps narrow the cause before anyone drives up the grade.

Quick checks before you call

  • Read the pressure gauge on the tank. A needle that swings fast between cut-in and cut-out usually points to a waterlogged tank or failing pressure switch..
  • Listen at the pump. A pump that runs constantly with nothing open suggests a leak somewhere downstream..
  • Check the breaker for the well pump and reset it once. If it trips again, leave it off..
  • Look at the storage tank level if you have one, since a low tank can mimic a pump failure..
  • Close every tap and watch the gauge for a slow fall, which usually means a hidden leak..

Septic tanks, setbacks and slow drains

.

The county septic plan says onsite systems are used almost exclusively outside municipal sewer boundaries, and the county Department of Environmental Health regulates them. It does not say every home here is on septic, so confirm yours. A septic home with gurgling fixtures may need its tank checked, more than a drain cable.

The plan also sets a 100-foot setback between a private well or spring and the septic tank and dispersal field. It bans cesspools and notes that older nonconforming systems cluster in the Lexington Basin, where many lots are under half an acre. If you own an older home, a camera and a pumping record tell us far more than a guess does.

Hard water and well testing

.

Valley Water says county groundwater averages over 250 mg/L hardness, while its treated surface water averages under 120. If your home draws from a well, expect harder water and ask us about water softener and filtration when scale clogs aerators or coats a heater element.

Valley Water also says domestic well water is not covered by federal or state quality rules, so the owner is responsible for testing and the agency encourages a yearly test at a state-certified lab. Test before you buy treatment equipment, so the system fits the water you actually have.

Fire season, private tanks and your plumbing

.

The county wildfire annex says nearly the whole area is rated high or extreme risk and that private firefighting tanks may not be full. Responders worry about reliable water supply, which is a good reason to keep your own tank plumbing, valves and fittings in working order.

A corroded valve or leaking outlet on a storage tank can drain the supply you counted on. We can inspect the plumbing on a tank and repair leaks, and a leak-finding visit can trace a slow loss on the line feeding it. Defensible space work is separate, and the county fire department offers a defensible space inspection at no charge for that.
