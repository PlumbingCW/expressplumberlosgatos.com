Sewer backup: what to do first

Sewage rising in a shower, tub or floor drain cannot wait for morning, so our dispatch line is open 24/7. Until a plumber is on site, the goal is to stop adding water to the pipe and keep the wet area clear.

  1. Stop all water use. No flushing, no washing machine, no dishwasher, no showers..
  2. Call West Valley Sanitation District. Its crews check the district main first and there is no extra charge for the visit..
  3. Keep children and pets out of the wet area, and keep sewage off hands and shoes..
  4. Call (855) 575-2890 if the district finds its main clear or says the blockage is on your side of the line..

Tell the dispatcher which fixture backed up first. The lowest drain in the house, often a basement or ground-floor shower, is usually the first place a main line blockage shows itself.

Main line clog or just one slow drain?

.

A single slow sink points to that fixture's own trap or branch pipe. A main line clog behaves differently because every drain downstream of the blockage is affected at once.

  • The toilet gurgles or bubbles when the washing machine drains..
  • Water from a shower rises around the floor drain after you flush..
  • More than one fixture is slow or backed up, usually on the lowest floor first..
  • A cleanout cap in the yard or garage shows standing water when removed..

Two or more of these together mean the blockage sits in the main. A plunger will not reach it, and chemical drain openers can sit in the pipe and burn whoever opens the cleanout next.

Gurgling in the tub when the washer drains usually means the main line. Call (855) 575-2890 and tell us which fixtures are backing up.

Who owns which part of the sewer lateral?

.

West Valley Sanitation District, which is based in Campbell, serves Los Gatos. Its code makes the upper lateral, from the house to the property line cleanout, the owner's responsibility, while the lower lateral from the cleanout to the public main belongs to the district.

District crews check their main and, where a cleanout exists, clear the lateral between that cleanout and the main. Anything beyond that needs a plumber. If your property has no cleanout, or nobody knows where it is, the owner is responsible for clearing the line all the way to the main, so locating or adding a cleanout is often the first job.

Houses along the Highway 17 corridor in Los Gatos and Campbell fall in the same district, which is based in Campbell and works in Santa Clara County, so the cleanout rule is the same on both sides. We clear lines in ZIP codes 95030, 95031, 95032 and 95033.

How a main line is cleared from the cleanout

.

We pull the cleanout cap slowly, since a full line can release under pressure. A powered drum machine then feeds a cable with a cutting head down the lateral, and the head grinds through grease, paper and fine roots until water drains freely.

The camera comes next when the cause is not obvious. A clog that returns within weeks usually has a reason, such as a sag in the pipe, a broken joint or a root mass that the cable only opens a channel through. Main sewer cleaning, sewer drain cleaning and sewer backup can sound like separate jobs, but the method is the same and the cleanout is the entry point for all three.

Why does the same line keep backing up?

.

Roots enter through joints in older clay and cast iron laterals, looking for moisture. Grease cools and coats the pipe wall until paper snags on it. Wipes sold as flushable do not break down, and the district's public message is to flush only toilet paper and human waste.

A line that clogs twice in a year deserves a look with a camera, which our sewer inspection work uses to measure the damage before anyone digs. If the pipe is cracked or sagging, sewer line repair replaces the failed section instead of clearing it again and again.

Backflow devices and the November 2026 cleanout date

.

The district requires an owner-funded backflow protective device when the lowest fixture floor sits less than one foot above the nearest upstream manhole. If you have one, check it every three to six months, because its flap can close during a blockage and hold wastewater in the house.

The code also ties continued district upkeep of existing lower laterals to cleanouts and laterals installed before November 1, 2026 that were permitted, built to standard and safely accessible. A cleanout buried under a planter or added without a permit may not qualify, so ask the district about yours. Work on a private upper lateral needs a permit, and we also dispatch into Campbell when the district office there is your first call.
