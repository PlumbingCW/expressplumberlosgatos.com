Broken faucet or supply valve: shut it off

A broken valve or a faucet that will not close can flood a cabinet in minutes. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, and you can slow the water right away with a few turns.

  1. Find the two small oval handles under the sink, called angle stops, and turn each clockwise until it stops..
  2. If water keeps coming, the stop itself has failed. Shut the main water valve, also clockwise, at the meter or where the line enters the house..
  3. Open the lowest faucet in the house to drain pressure from the pipes..
  4. Empty the cabinet, put a bucket under the leak, and call (855) 575-2890..

The valve under the sink broke or will not close

.

Angle stops sit untouched for years, then fail the first time anyone turns them. The stem packing dries out, the washer crumbles, or the handle spins without closing anything. Old stops of chrome-plated brass and multi-turn gate design are the usual culprits.

When a stop fails, the fix is to shut the main, cut out the old valve and solder or compression-fit a new quarter-turn ball valve. That is also the right moment to replace the braided supply lines, since they are about the same age as the valve.

Shower valve and tub faucet leaks

.

A showerhead that drips after the water is off often has a worn diverter or a cartridge behind the handle. Tub spouts drip when the cartridge seats poorly. A drip that you can hear inside the wall during a shower deserves attention because it can wet framing out of sight.

Replacing a cartridge usually means opening the wall trim, not the tile. If the valve body behind the wall is corroded or the handle trim has no access, we explain the options before cutting anything.

Water that drains slowly from the shower or tub

.

Slow shower and tub drains are more often hair and soap in the trap than a failed fixture. We pull the stopper or strainer, remove the buildup, and test the flow. If it clears and returns, drain cleaning with a cable reaches past the trap into the branch line.

A gurgle in the tub when other fixtures drain points further downstream.

Los Gatos hard water and your fixtures

.

San Jose Water reports three source types, and their hardness differs. Mountain surface water averages 151 ppm, groundwater 288 ppm and Valley Water treated supply 99 ppm. Which one serves your home varies, so test your tap.

In harder supply, white crust builds on aerators and inside cartridges, and handles stiffen long before they leak. The utility advises cleaning aerators regularly and running the tap 30 seconds to 2 minutes if water has sat for several hours. If crust returns quickly, water softener and filtration is worth asking about.

Outdoor spigots and jetted tubs

.

A hose bib that drips behind the handle usually needs a new packing nut or stem. A spigot that leaks inside the wall needs shutting off at its interior valve, if one exists, until it is replaced.

Jetted tubs hide their plumbing behind the access panel, and a leaking pump union or jet fitting needs that panel opened. Tell us the tub brand when you call.
