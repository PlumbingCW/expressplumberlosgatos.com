A San Jose neighborhood with its own rules

.

Cambrian Park sits in south San Jose, with Willow Glen to the north, Campbell to the northwest, Los Gatos to the south and west, and Almaden to the east. Its center is Cambrian Park Plaza, which opened in 1953 where Camden Avenue meets Union Avenue. Because the address is a San Jose one, the permit office, the sewer main and the spill hotline all belong to the city, not to Los Gatos.

That matters the first time something goes wrong. A caller on a Cambrian street needs to know which utility sends the water bill, who owns which stretch of sewer pipe, and which city issues the permit for a new water heater. We sort those three questions out first, then fix the pipe.

Before we arrive in Cambrian Park

If water is spreading or a drain is backing up, a few minutes of work on your end limits the damage. Many older Cambrian homes have a main shutoff valve on the outside wall or near the garage, so look there first.

  1. Turn the main water valve clockwise until it stops. If the valve is stiff or corroded, do not force it, and tell the dispatcher..
  2. Switch off power to wet rooms at the breaker panel, and stay out of standing water near outlets or appliances..
  3. Clear the area under the leak or backup, lift rugs and move boxes, and do not run sinks, showers or the washer..
  4. Call (855) 575-2890, describe what you see, and mention if sewage is coming up. The 24/7 line is staffed for exactly this..

Who owns the sewer pipe when it backs up?

.

In San Jose the city maintains the sewer main in the street. The property owner is responsible for the whole lateral, the pipe from your house to that main. The city does cover the lower lateral from an approved Y access point near the property line, as a courtesy, but not every home has one.

That split decides who pays, so find the clog before you call anyone else. A sewer camera shows whether the blockage sits in your lateral, at the Y, or in the main. About 80 percent of occupied households in the Cambrian Park census-designated place are owner-occupied, so most homeowners here carry that lateral themselves.

If the camera shows the problem is in the city main, San Jose lists 408-794-1900 for sewer spills, odors and overflows. If it is in your lateral, the city tells owners to hire a plumber, and cleaning the sewer line usually comes first.

Which sewer district covers your street?

.

Cambrian Park has an unusual history. In 2012 Campbell annexed a large part of the unincorporated Cambrian Park pocket under an agreement with San Jose, and the rest is expected to join San Jose later. When a neighboring city annexes land inside West Valley Sanitation District, that land is detached and the city takes over sewer collection.

So a home that looks like Cambrian Park may sit in San Jose or in Campbell, and the sewer provider can differ from the neighbor's. Confirm your address with the city or district before you assume who to call about a main-line problem. We can still clear your own lateral either way.

Check your water bill before a leak call

.

San Jose has three water providers, Great Oaks Water Company, San Jose Water Company and the city's own municipal system. The municipal system serves only North San Jose, Alviso, Evergreen, Edenvale and Coyote Valley, so it is not your supplier in Cambrian. Great Oaks serves parts of south San Jose, while San Jose Water covers other parts.

Which one reaches your street is not something we can promise from a map. Look at your bill, because the supplier owns the meter and everything up to it. A leak on your side of the meter is yours to fix, and a water service line repair is the usual answer when the buried pipe is the culprit.

Drain cleaning and water line repair in older Cambrian homes

.

Older Cambrian kitchen and bath lines collect grease, hair and roots over the decades. A cable or high-pressure jetting pass scours the pipe wall when a snake only punches a hole. Our drain cleaning work starts at the nearest cleanout when one exists.

Water lines deserve a look as well. Some older homes have galvanized steel inside the walls or at the valve, and that pipe narrows from rust over the decades. Low pressure at every faucet, orange water after a long idle, or a soggy patch near the meter box all point the same way.

Spot repairs can buy time, but a long run of rusted pipe may be cheaper to replace than to chase.

What to check on a wet spot in the yard

.

Before you call, a quick walk around the property tells the dispatcher a lot.

Yard leak checklist

  • Shut every fixture off, then read the meter. If the dial still moves, water is escaping somewhere..
  • Look for a green, soft or sunken strip of lawn running from the meter toward the house..
  • Listen at the hose bib and the main valve for hissing with all taps closed..
  • Note whether pressure dropped at every faucet or only one fixture..
  • Check the meter box lid for standing water, and keep the lid clear..

Water heater swaps need a San Jose permit

.

San Jose requires a plumbing permit to install or replace a water heater, and an electrical permit may also be needed depending on the type. Single-family and duplex permits are applied for online at SJPermits.org, and the city recommends a licensed contractor. After the install, an inspection is booked online or by phone.

The permit review checks the installation, the venting and the seismic strapping, which counts in earthquake country. Unpermitted work can lead to denied insurance claims, so ask any installer who pulls the permit. If your tank is failing, our tank repair visit tells you whether a new anode rod or thermostat is enough or the tank itself has gone.
