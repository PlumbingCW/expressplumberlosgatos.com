Backed-up drain emergency: what to do now

A drain that backs up for the second or third time is an emergency when sewage is at a floor drain or tub. Los Gatos Express Plumbing runs 24/7 dispatch, and a plumber can assess the line the same day when schedules allow.

  1. Stop running water. Do not flush, run the dishwasher or start laundry until the line is clear..
  2. Keep people and pets off wet flooring. Backed-up sewage carries bacteria, so wear gloves if you must touch anything..
  3. Find your cleanout, a capped pipe near the house or at the property line, and leave the cap closed unless a plumber asks you to open it..
  4. Do not pour drain chemicals into a standing backup. Call (855) 575-2890 for dispatch..

Chemical cleaners sit in the pipe and can splash back on a plumber. Skipping them also keeps the camera view clear.

How hydro jetting differs from a cable

.

A drain cable, also called a snake, is a spinning steel cable that punches a hole through a clog. It clears the path, but grease and scale left on the pipe wall can build the blockage again within weeks.

A jetter sends high-pressure water through a hose with a nozzle that points backward and forward. The water scours the pipe wall and flushes debris downstream. It is the better tool for heavy grease, built-up scale and fine roots, and for lines that keep clogging. A cable is still the right first tool for a simple soft clog.

When the snake got stuck or the clog returned

.

Most jetting calls begin with a sentence like, I had it snaked and it came back. That pattern is usually a coating problem. The cable opens a channel, and the sludge along the walls slowly closes it again.

Roots do the same thing. A cable cuts the strands inside the pipe, but the root mass at the joint regrows. Jetting can clear root fibers and the grease that holds them, though it does not stop roots from entering a cracked joint. If roots keep returning, the pipe itself needs a look.

A camera comes first, always

.

A camera matters even more for jetting. A sewer camera shows the pipe material, the clog and any cracks, bellies or offset joints before water pressure goes in.

That order protects you. High-pressure water is the wrong tool for a collapsed or cracked pipe, because it can push through weak spots and wash soil into the line. It can also turn a small crack into a larger one.

If the camera shows damage, we explain it and talk through options, which may include sewer line repair instead of jetting. A separate sewer camera inspection is available when you want the pipe documented on its own.

Your side of the sewer lateral in Los Gatos

.

West Valley Sanitation District serves Los Gatos, and its code makes the upper lateral, from the building to the cleanout at the property line, the owner's responsibility. The lower lateral to the public main belongs to the district. If there is no cleanout, or you cannot find it, the district says the owner is responsible for clearing the line all the way to the main.

That matters for jetting. A cleanout gives access for a hose and a camera, so finding yours saves time. District crews check the main and, where a cleanout exists, clear the lateral beyond it.

Anything on your side needs a plumber, and work on a private upper lateral requires a permit. Ask the District or Town if you are unsure.

A jetting truck needs room to park and a clear hose path to the cleanout, which is worth mentioning if your street is narrow or steep near Highway 17. Jetting calls from Santa Clara County ZIP codes 95030, 95031, 95032 and 95033 are dispatched from the same number.

Grease, wipes and what not to flush

.

Kitchen grease is a frequent cause of repeat clogs. It cools in the pipe and sticks. West Valley Sanitation District's public message is to flush only toilet paper, pee and poop, and to keep so-called flushable wipes out of the toilet. Wipes do not break down like paper, and they catch on rough pipe.

A practical habit is to pour cooking fat into a jar, let it set, and put it in the trash. Wipe pans with a paper towel before washing. If you run a restaurant, the District requires a grease control device approved by its manager, and a jetting plan should fit that setup. Our drain cleaning page covers the routine side.
